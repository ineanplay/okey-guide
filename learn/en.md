# OKEY: from recognizing tiles to finishing a hand

[English](en.md) · [简体中文](zh.md) · [Türkçe](tr.md) · [Deutsch](de.md)

Use this lesson for four-player manual practice: classic OKEY first, then 101 Okey. Loading an automatic table follows its model instructions. The lesson covers basic play and a stated practice scoring scheme, not a club's entire tournament code.

## 1. Prepare the set and table

Use 106 playing tiles: two copies of numbers 1-13 in four colors (104), plus two false jokers. Set spare tiles aside. Play anticlockwise; choose a dealer, with the player on the dealer's right starting.

For a simple teaching deal, mix tiles face down and expose a random ordinary numbered tile as the indicator. Leave it on the table. Give the starter 15 tiles and the others 14 each; leave the rest face down to draw. Return a false joker and choose again if it is selected as indicator. The starter discards without drawing. This is a practice dealing method; organized games may use an agreed dice-and-stack procedure.

## 2. Identify the joker before sorting

The indicator is the **gösterge**. The next number of its color is **okey**, wrapping from 13 to 1; both matching numbered tiles are wild. A **sahte okey** is not freely wild: it represents the ordinary identity of that numbered tile.

| Indicator | Wild tiles | False joker represents |
|---|---|---|
| Blue 8 | Blue 9 | Ordinary Blue 9 |
| Black 13 | Black 1 | Ordinary Black 1 |

Sort by color and number, then look for equal numbers in different colors. Keep wild tiles visible to yourself and assign a definite identity when checking a group.

## 3. Recognize combinations

| Combination | Valid example | Common error |
|---|---|---|
| Run of at least 3 | Red 3-4-5; Blue 6-7-8-9 | Mixing colors |
| Set of 3 or 4 | Red 7, Blue 7, Black 7 | Repeating a color in one set |
| Seven pairs in classic OKEY | Seven same-color, same-number pairs | Treating Red 5 and Blue 5 as a pair |

Common classic rules allow 12-13-1 of one color, but not 13-1-2. The 101 baseline below does not allow 12-13-1. Each physical tile belongs to exactly one group.

## 4. Take one, then discard one

After the starter's initial discard, take either one face-down tile or the previous player's latest discard. Arrange your hand and discard to your right. You cannot choose from any player's older discards. Classic OKEY groups remain concealed until the finish.

An ordinary turn holds 15 tiles after taking and 14 after discarding. To finish, the retained 14 must partition into legal groups or seven pairs. Discard the fifteenth and show your arrangement for checking.

## 5. A complete tile example

Here the indicator is Black 4, so Black 5 is wild. All tiles below are ordinary.

| Your 14 tiles | Count |
|---|---:|
| Red 2-3-4-5 | 4 |
| Blue 6-7-8 | 3 |
| Black 10-11-12 | 3 |
| Red 1, Blue 1, Yellow 1 | 3 |
| Loose Black 3 | 1 |

Draw Blue 9: you now have 15. Extend the blue run, discard Black 3, and retain `4+4+3+3=14` tiles in valid groups. The same physical Blue 7 cannot also be counted in a separate set.

## 6. Record a teaching session

For an initial lesson, explicitly agree on three hands: finisher +1, others 0; no score when the stock is exhausted and play cannot continue under the agreed procedure; rotate the starter each hand. These are practice points, with no special-finish bonuses, not universal OKEY scoring.

| Record | A | B | C | D |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Start | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Hand 1: B finishes | 0 | +1 | 0 | 0 |
| Hand 2: A finishes | +1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Hand 3: B finishes | 0 | +1 | 0 | 0 |
| Final | 1 | 2 | 0 | 0 |

Before regular play, write down starting points, score direction, pairs, indicator bonus, joker finish, draws and penalties. Do not treat this teaching score as the regular tariff.

## 7. Try 101 Okey next

Use the same 106 tiles and joker method, but deal 22 to the starter and 21 to everyone else. The starter may open legally before discarding. Later turns take a tile, make permitted melds or extensions, and discard. Even the final turn requires a discard: do not put down every tile with none left to discard.

A standard first opening places legal runs or sets totaling at least 101 in one turn. Add represented numbers; wild tiles take their assigned number. Tiles added to someone else's existing meld do not count toward your opening threshold.

| Opening | Value |
|---|---:|
| Red 10-11-12-13 | 46 |
| Blue 7-8-9 | 24 |
| Three differently colored 11s | 33 |
| Total | **103: standard opening met** |

After opening, play further groups or permitted extensions. The previous discard cannot be stored for later: in this baseline, use it immediately; before opening it must form part of your qualifying first opening. Do not reclaim or move exposed jokers as in Rummikub.

The alternative pair route begins with at least five identical pairs in one turn in the cited baseline. After opening pairs, do not start new runs or sets, though you may extend other players' existing runs or sets. Practice the ordinary opening route first. Special finishes, pair scoring, unopened-hand penalties and multipliers need a separate agreement. “101” is not the tile count.

## 8. Check yourself

1. With indicator Red 13, can a false joker freely replace Black 6? No: it represents ordinary Red 1.
2. Can you open with 98 and complete the 101 threshold later? Not for a standard opening; qualify in the same turn.
3. Holding Blue 7-8, can you draw Blue 9 and put down all three to finish 101? No: a last discard is still required.
